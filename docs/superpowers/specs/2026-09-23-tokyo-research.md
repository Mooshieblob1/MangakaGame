# Tokyo and Japan research ledger — Sub-project 3

Researched: 2026-09-23. Setting: 1 April 1996 onward.
Companion: [Staff and Studio design](2026-09-23-staff-studio-design.md).

The user requires relevant design work to be grounded in real Japanese data
for Tokyo. Prefer primary Japanese sources. Distinguish the date a document
was published from the period its figures describe. Do not turn a current
price, national rule or residential average into an exact 1996 Tokyo studio
quote. No final gameplay numbers have been validated by this research alone.

Classification:

- **H — historical evidence:** describes the relevant past period directly.
- **P — proxy/context:** modern or adjacent-period evidence with limited fit.
- **G — game choice:** authored balance, fictional catalog, simplified rule
  or user-selected premise. Cite the benchmark without claiming the choice
  itself was measured.

## R1. Geography

Tokyo Metropolitan Government describes Greater Tokyo as Tokyo plus Saitama,
Chiba and Kanagawa; its special-ward system has 23 wards. This supports the
map's outer boundary and distinction between Tokyo proper and nearby urban
areas. [TMG geography and history](https://www.english.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/w/000-101-007591).

**P:** current official geographic description. **G:** the selected playable
nodes, six eligible starting wards and particular fictional property anchors.
Use 1996 place names and verify infrastructure opening dates when authoring
routes. Do not infer within-ward walking distance from administrative labels.

## R2. Historical Tokyo wage floor

MHLW's historical regional minimum-wage table gives Tokyo hourly figures of
¥650 for fiscal 1995 and ¥664 for fiscal 1996, then ¥679 in 1997. These are
annual table values, not proof of the rate in force on 1 April of each year.
[MHLW historical hourly data](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/shingi/2003/12/s1202-3h.html).

**H:** historical Tokyo hourly benchmark. **G:** proposed ¥700/hour hiring
floor and ¥122k full-time monthly floor, using 40*52/12 hours. This does not
establish typical manga-assistant wages, salaried benefits or living costs.
Exact effective-date scheduling needs additional historical data if implemented
as a literal legal wage-floor timeline.

## R3. Hours, premium pay and dismissal

MHLW describes an eight-hour/40-hour ordinary framework, overtime premium of
at least 25% and statutory-holiday premium of at least 35%, with exceptions
and procedures. [MHLW hours and premiums](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/bunya/roudoukijun/faq_kijyungyosei07.html).
Its dismissal guidance includes 30 days' notice or equivalent notice pay;
paying notice alone does not establish that a dismissal is lawful.
[MHLW employment termination guidance](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/seisakunitsuite/bunya/koyou_roudou/roudouseisaku/chushoukigyou/keiyakushuryo_rule.html).

**P:** current explanations, not a reconstructed 1996 legal code. **G:** use
these recognizable structures for simple staff scheduling and costs. The
prototype treats booked rest-day conventions uniformly and omits detailed
legal exceptions, disputes, agreements and historical phase-in. Do not present
these simplifications as exact legal compliance or typical mangaka hours.

## R4. Historical rent benchmark

Arakawa Ward's March 2013 external audit describes local conditions in 1992:
residential monthly rents around ¥2,000–4,000/m²; a ¥3,000/m² midpoint produces
¥150k/month for 50m². See printed page 5, PDF page 8.
[Arakawa official audit](https://www.city.arakawa.tokyo.jp/documents/4662/houkatukansa.pdf).

**H:** retrospective 1992 Arakawa housing benchmark. **P:** comparison for
fictional small workrooms in 1996, not commercial lease data or an all-Tokyo
average. **G:** tier prices, deposits, utilities and location modifiers.
Retain this limitation when quoting the property catalog. Larger/central
commercial offices need their own period-specific benchmarks before claiming
realistic district premiums.

**User-confirmed exception:** the starting workroom is in the mangaka's parents'
house and costs ¥0 rent. No rental-market estimate overrides that premise.

## R5. Local transport

Toei's historical fare-revision table records the 1995 scale: initial ¥170
through 4km, ¥210 through 9km, ¥250 through 15km, then ¥50 per extra 6km.
[Toei historical statistics, transport fares](https://www.kotsu.metro.tokyo.jp/about/information/statistics/pdf/statistics_2018_08.pdf).

**H, provisional retrieval:** values were available in the indexed text of
the operator's archival PDF; direct retrieval returned an unavailable/404
document during this session. Recheck an accessible official copy/table
before treating the numerical catalog as fully verified production data.
**G:** using those bands across the game's simplified network. They are not
exact fares for mixed-operator trips. Walking/cycling radius 3km, whole-hour
rounding and estimated route lengths are also game choices. No modern IC
discounts or later railway services should appear in a 1996 itinerary.

## R6. Printing structure and modern copy price

Seven-Eleven's official FAQ, updated July 2025, lists monochrome copying at
¥10 per printed side for supported paper sizes. This is not a historical
1996 quote. [Seven-Eleven copy prices](https://faq.sej.co.jp/article/?knowledge_id=cidqbq4vr0h8m5o3sn30).

Taiyou Shuppan's current doujin product pages distinguish page counts, paper,
run sizes and printing sets. Their menus support quoting by pages and quantity,
not charging one flat fee for any book.
[Printer's basic product menu](https://www.taiyoushuppan.co.jp/doujin/product/kihon/index.php),
[short-run set example](https://www.taiyoushuppan.co.jp/doujin/product/set/o-novel2023.php).

**P:** modern process/price comparisons. **G:** all 1996 game print formulas,
assembly allowance, turnaround, presentation ratings and retail cover price.
Count physical pages, imposed printed sides and covers explicitly. For a
100-page A5 book, the proposed copy formula costs ¥520/book; do not silently
apply ¥10 to a whole book or charge every A5 page as a separate A4 impression.
Do not transplant present-day smartphone/network printing into 1996.

## R7. Convention geography and history

The organiser's chronology places Comic Market 50 at Tokyo Big Sight in
Ariake on 3–4 August 1996, with 18,000 circles and 350,000 attendees across
the event. [Comic Market official chronology](https://www.comiket.co.jp/archives/Chronology.html).

**H:** event date, location and reported scale. **G:** fictional analogue,
player booth traffic, sale probability and fee. Whole-event attendance is
not unique prospective customers at the player's table. Entry rules for
visitors and exhibitors are distinct. This source does not verify our free
community events or proposed 1996 exhibitor fee.

## R8. Exhibitor fees and table scale

COMITIA 125's application sheet for 19 August 2018 lists ¥5,300 for one space,
including half a table, a chair and three passes.
[COMITIA 125 organiser application sheet](https://www.comitia.co.jp/pdf/125.pdf).

**P:** 2018 original-manga event, not a 1996 fee. **G:** ¥0/¥5k/¥8k catalog
fees and staff/carrying limits. Actual organiser rules can restrict corporate
exhibitors; fictional events must explicitly declare their own eligibility.
Free events waive the participation fee, not automatically travel or staff time.

## R9. Consumer credit and lender names

SMBC Consumer Finance's history records consumer lending from 1963, Tokyo
expansion in 1969 and an automated credit provision system in 1983.
[Consumer lender's history](https://www.smbc-cf.com/english/corporate/history.html).
JACCS records credit cards from 1970 and reducing card-cash-loan interest to
18% in February 1997. [JACCS history](https://www.jaccs.co.jp/en/corporate/about/history/).

**H:** evidence that these credit categories existed before/near the start.
The 1997 rate must not be described as a verified 1996 quote. Neither history
establishes which product was most common among manga creators. **G:** game
APRs, limits, eligibility, repayment formulas and incorporation unlock.

Japan Finance Corporation's institutional history dates that organisation to
2008, with predecessor institutions earlier. Use a fictional period-appropriate
lender at the 1996 start, not the later institutional name.
[JFC institutional history, 2025 report](https://www.jfc.go.jp/n/english/report/pdf/jfc2025e_5.pdf).
Eligibility for real sole-trader business borrowing must not be inferred from
our deliberate incorporation gate.

## R10. Company capital and the 2006 boundary

The Ministry of Justice's explanation of the Companies Act contrasts the old
¥10m kabushiki-kaisha and ¥3m yugen-kaisha minimum capital with removal of the
minimum. Its companion Q&A dates enforcement to 1 May 2006.
[MOJ company-law comparison](https://www.moj.go.jp/content/000058411.pdf),
[MOJ commencement Q&A](https://www.moj.go.jp/MINJI/minji92.html).

**H:** old thresholds and reform date. **G:** selecting the small-company
analogue, net-assets eligibility simplification, ¥200k setup and ¥20k/month
administration. Capital is retained business funding, unlike fees. Do not
equate the game's net-assets check with a complete legal incorporation process,
or backdate the later godo-kaisha form to 1996. The detailed intermediate
exemption regime is outside this milestone's simplified company model.

## Remaining evidence gaps

Before marketing numerical catalogs as period-accurate, verify: accessible
1996 copier/printer price lists; district-specific
commercial leases; actual assistant compensation; period internet plans;
1996 exhibitor fees; appropriate real credit ranges. These gaps do not prevent
using clearly labelled game estimates in the prototype. They must not be
filled by presenting current prices as historical observations.

Future evidence entries should record exact source title/URL, observation and
publication dates, geography, units, extracted fact, game adaptation and its
limits. Save only necessary facts/brief excerpts, not whole copyrighted sources.


## R11. Implementation verification, 23 September 2026

The 2018/2019 transport PDF links returned 404 on direct retrieval. The
accessible **Tokyo Metropolitan Bureau of Transportation, 2022 statistics,
Other principal matters**, table 3, PDF page 15, confirms the 1 March 1995
subway initial fare of ¥170 and the distance-band note: up to 4 km initial,
9 km ¥210, 15 km ¥250, then ¥50 per additional 6 km. This closes the earlier
accessibility gap in R5. [Official 2022 statistics, p.15](https://www.kotsu.metro.tokyo.jp/about/information/statistics/pdf/statistics_2022_08.pdf#page=15).

**H:** that historical fare schedule. **G:** applying one schedule across the
whole Greater Tokyo map, the 1.25 route-distance factor, rounded municipal
points, walking/cycling threshold and hour rounding. The schematic is not a
street map, contemporary fare quote or exact historical journey planner.
[GSI map reference](https://maps.gsi.go.jp/vector/) is available for future
address-level refinement; the prototype's approximate points are game inputs,
not a surveyed dataset extracted from that service.

The map uses **Urawa**, rather than backdating the name Saitama City to 1996.
The latter was formed by the 2001 merger of Urawa, Omiya and Yono.
[Municipal merger record](https://www.city.saitama.lg.jp/006/012/001/010/006/p023087_d/fil/46b32ae4047.pdf).
Ariake is a separate venue point for the major summer/winter conventions;
they do not use Koto's ward-centre point as though it were Tokyo Big Sight.

Initial rent remains exactly zero by player instruction. Four property choices
per tier, salaries, loan APRs, running costs and print quotes are **G**, informed
by the earlier **H/P** references, not advertised as measured 1996 prices.
