The remaining roadmap covers newcomer awards, anime adaptations, milestones,
starting difficulty, balance and polish. Existing publishing design also points

merchandise licensing is now confirmed within this sub-project (decision 16).

## Decision 1 — award coverage

Confirmed: **both newcomer submission contests and annual awards for established
published manga**. Contests can help launch careers; annual awards recognize
published series. Exact eligibility, judging, timing and rewards are not yet set.

## Decision 2 — judging priorities

Confirmed: primarily quality, originality and award-specific fit,
against an actual eligible field, with bounded jury variation. Popularity can
matter for designated reader-voted awards, without making every award a sales
leaderboard. Strong work should improve chances without guaranteeing a win.

## Decision 3 — preparing newcomer contest entries

Confirmed: create a dedicated one-shot through the normal production
system, using time and available staff. An existing eligible, unpublished
one-shot may also be submitted. Contest-specific eligibility and deadlines apply;
submission itself does not grant free production or guaranteed publication.

## Decision 4 — newcomer contest rewards

Confirmed: tiered results, with modest recognition for shortlisted
entries and larger prizes, reputation gains and publisher interest for top
placements. Strong results can open editor meetings or publication offers;
serialization is not guaranteed. Exact amounts and account attribution remain
to be decided. These are agreed game mechanics, not researched contest rules.

## Decision 5 — feedback on contest results

Confirmed: every entry receives brief qualitative feedback on its
strengths and weaknesses, with more detailed editorial comments for shortlisted
and prize-winning work. Feedback should distinguish craft, award fit and strong
competition without exposing exact judging scores or promising future success.
This is an agreed player-facing game system, not a claim about real contests.

## Decision 6 — revising and resubmitting unsuccessful entries

Confirmed: allow revision through normal production, costing time
and effort, followed by submission to a later eligible contest. Eligibility
must account for that contest's rules and the work's submission, publication
and prize history. An unresolved entry cannot be entered in another contest
simultaneously. Revision improves the work without guaranteeing a better result.
This is an agreed game rule; historical contest rules need separate research.

## Decision 7 — annual award nominations

Confirmed: eligible published manga are considered automatically by
the game's award system, with no recurring player application. Consideration
does not guarantee a nomination. Helper-Chan announces shortlisted nominations
and results. Quality-first judging and award-specific eligibility still apply.
This is an agreed gameplay abstraction, not a claim about real award procedures.

## Decision 8 — effects of annual awards

Confirmed: a win grants lasting career/series prestige and a temporary
increase in reader discovery and sales potential. Effects scale with the award's
stature and the series' existing reach; repeat recognition has diminishing
commercial returns. Awards can increase adaptation interest without guaranteeing
an offer. Nominations provide smaller benefits. Exact tuning remains open.
These are agreed game mechanics, not measured real-world economic effects.

## Decision 9 — how anime adaptation opportunities arise

Confirmed: both incoming offers and optional active pitching through
industry contacts. Popularity, critical recognition, adaptation suitability and
available source material influence interest. Pitching costs time and effort
and does not guarantee an offer. Any eventual deal remains subject to the
creator's rights, employment authority and contract terms; those details are
still to be designed. These are agreed game mechanics, not researched industry
contract procedures.

## Decision 10 — creator involvement in adaptations

Confirmed: choose an involvement level for each adaptation, within
the authority allowed by its contract: hands-off, occasional consultation, or
close supervision. More involvement consumes creator time otherwise available
for manga production and gives more opportunities to influence fidelity and
creative decisions, without guaranteeing quality or success. Contractual
approval rights remain distinct from time spent consulting.
These are agreed game mechanics, not claims about standard Japanese contracts.

## Decision 11 — comparing adaptation offers

Confirmed: offers have distinct tradeoffs in compensation, studio
track record and genre fit, production schedule, planned scope and creative
approval rights. Present a readable comparison, with uncertain outcomes clearly
separated from promised terms. Players may accept an available offer, decline,
or wait for another opportunity; multiple simultaneous offers are not guaranteed.
A more prestigious or better-paying offer is not automatically best on every
dimension. These are agreed game abstractions; contract details and historical
industry grounding remain to be researched.

## Decision 12 — adaptation production setbacks

Confirmed: adaptations can encounter delays, creative disputes and
occasional cancellations. Risk is influenced by studio reliability, schedule
pressure and project circumstances, with warning signs and opportunities to
respond where the creator's contract permits. Sound choices reduce risk without
eliminating it. Helper-Chan presents important developments and available choices.
These are agreed game mechanics; frequencies and consequences remain untuned.

## Decision 13 — adaptations catching up to the manga

Confirmed: available source material constrains adaptation planning.
When a project risks catching up, present context-appropriate options such as
ending the season at a suitable stopping point and waiting for more chapters,
adding original side stories, or developing an anime-original continuation or
ending. Availability depends on production stage, agreed scope and contractual
authority. Each route has schedule, fidelity and audience-reception tradeoffs;
original material is not automatically bad. These are agreed game mechanics,
not claims about standard industry practice in any particular era.

## Decision 14 — anime reception and the original manga

Confirmed: distinguish anime reception from the manga's own quality
and reputation. Successful adaptations bring new readers, stronger backlist
sales and possible lasting fans. Poor adaptations usually yield smaller gains,
with limited, recoverable reputational spillover in severe cases; existing manga
fans are comparatively resilient. Exposure can still bring readers even when
reception is mixed. Effects depend on reach and reception rather than a single
success/failure switch. These are agreed game mechanics, not measured effects.

## Decision 15 — follow-up anime seasons

Confirmed: follow-up seasons are separate opportunities, influenced
by audience demand, prior adaptation performance, available source material and
production-partner availability. Players can seek or receive renewed offers,
subject to existing rights and contractual commitments. Success improves renewal
prospects without guaranteeing a sequel, and later seasons can have different
terms or production partners where rights permit. These are agreed game
mechanics; period-specific industry details remain to be researched.

## Decision 16 — merchandise scope

Confirmed: include lightweight merchandise licensing deals in this
sub-project. Players approve eligible offers for products such as figures,
apparel and stationery, subject to rights and employment authority. Partners
handle manufacturing and inventory; the game tracks deal income, demand and
reputation effects. Merchandise can arise from manga popularity without requiring
an anime. Contract economics and account attribution remain to be designed and
researched; this decision does not assume standard real-world royalty terms.

## Decision 17 — career milestone rewards

Confirmed: record milestones permanently in the career journal, with
Helper-Chan celebrating major firsts such as first publication, first award,
first anime and major readership milestones. Selected milestones can open
contextual events or opportunities, without automatic permanent skill or sales
bonuses. Track creator achievements separately from series and studio records
so the career continues to follow the protagonist after a move.

## Decision 18 — awards and adaptations contributing to iconic status

Confirmed: major awards and successful adaptations contribute bounded
cultural-impact gains alongside the existing manga publication and creative
breakthrough systems. Repeated similar achievements have diminishing returns;
merely signing deals does not grant impact. Preserve the existing cultural-impact
and fanbase requirements for iconic status, its permanence and the prodigy's
existing creative advantage. An award or adaptation never grants iconic status
automatically. Manga alone remains a viable route.

## Decision 19 — what broader difficulty settings change

Confirmed: keep the protagonist a prodigy on every difficulty and
primarily vary starting financial cushion, business pressure and recovery
allowances. Keep title-ownership rules as the separate, previously agreed
new-game choice and preserve the rent-free parents' home. Specific presets and
values remain open. This supersedes the roadmap's earlier starting-skill
scaling proposal; difficulty does not reduce the protagonist's starting skills.

## Decision 20 — choosing difficulty settings

Confirmed: offer Relaxed, Standard and Challenging presets, plus a
Custom option for independently choosing starting financial cushion, business
pressure and recovery assistance from bounded settings. Explain each setting's
effects in plain language. Title ownership remains a separate choice. Exact
values and the default preset remain to be specified. Mid-career changes are
covered by decision 23. Also include a Sandbox mode with configurable options, as
explicitly requested by the user. Its controls are confirmed in decision 21.

## Decision 21 — sandbox controls

Confirmed: provide individual toggles for unlimited personal funds,
unlimited business funds, instant production/construction, unlocking locations
and equipment, and disabling stress or deadline penalties. Players can combine
the options they want; choosing Sandbox does not require enabling every assist.
Keep personal and business accounts distinct even with unlimited funds.
Historical-era restrictions are covered by decision 22. Mid-career changes and
Sandbox achievement eligibility are covered by decision 23.

## Decision 22 — sandbox and historical-era restrictions

Confirmed: preserve historical progression by default, with a
separate Sandbox option to unlock future equipment and publishing channels
early. This option changes availability rather than moving the calendar or
firing future historical events. Keep it independent of ordinary location and
equipment progression unlocks. Era-specific availability and any exceptions
must be made clear in the UI.

## Decision 23 — changing difficulty during a career

Confirmed: allow changes to ongoing business difficulty and Sandbox
assists during a career, including enabling Sandbox on an existing career.
Starting funds and title-ownership rules remain new-game choices; changing a
preset does not award another starting balance or rewrite past rights and
transactions. Changes apply going forward and are saved with the career.
Turning assists off does not undo already completed work. Detailed handling of
early-unlocked assets remains to be specified.

User addition: **disable Steam achievements for Sandbox of any kind**, in
preparation for a Steam release. Starting in Sandbox, switching into it or
enabling any Sandbox assist makes that save and its subsequent copies permanently
ineligible for new Steam achievement unlocks. Disabling assists later does not
restore eligibility or allow retroactive unlocks for Sandbox accomplishments.
Display the consequence before activation and retain the eligibility flag across
saves, loads and exports. Previously earned Steam achievements remain intact;
independent clean careers and clean saves from before activation are unaffected.
Ordinary difficulty presets and bounded Custom settings remain eligible, subject
to any individual achievement's conditions. Preserve in-game milestone records,
Helper-Chan celebrations and associated gameplay events in Sandbox. Route future
Steam unlocks, including queued/offline awards, through the same eligibility
check. This is the game's achievement policy, not a claim about Steam rules;
the actual Steam integration needs separate implementation and verification.

## Decision 24 — Steam achievement coverage

Confirmed: combine natural career milestones with optional mastery
challenges, such as earning a major award or completing an especially successful
career. Avoid achievements requiring repeated restarts for rare random events.
The exact achievement list and thresholds remain to be designed; Sandbox is
ineligible for every Steam achievement regardless of category.

## Decision 25 — award and licensing income attribution

Confirmed: creator award prizes go to the named recipient's personal
account. Anime and merchandise licensing receipts use the existing business
income plus creator-share model, with recipients and splits explicitly shown
before acceptance and recorded in the deal. Exact percentages remain to be
specified; do not assume publishing's default percentage applies automatically.
Moving studios does not rewrite an existing deal's historical attribution or
accrued obligations. This is an agreed game convention, not a claim about
standard Japanese contracts or tax treatment.

illustrated conversation during quiet periods; do not add a player-initiated
desk interaction. Use the existing optional everyday-scene scheduling and

## Decision 28 — negotiating adaptation and merchandise offers

Confirmed: offer a small set of clear counteroffers, such as asking
for better payment, stronger creative approval rights or a different schedule
where relevant. Negotiating strength depends on the work's appeal, creator
standing and partner interest; a counteroffer may require a concession, be
rejected or cause talks to end. Explain foreseeable tradeoffs before selection.
Bound negotiation rounds to avoid repeated free attempts, and respect the
player's contractual and employment authority. These are agreed game rules,
not researched standard contract procedures.
