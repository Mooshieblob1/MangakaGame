# Mangaka Studio: Roadmap

Working title: **Mangaka Studio**. A real-time management sim in the vein of
Software Inc., but for manga. The player directs work around a starting prodigy
mangaka who can grow a studio across multiple locations. The player does not
necessarily play as that person, but events centre on them and the player's
perspective follows them if they leave a studio. Staff work semi-autonomously
toward assigned series. Built in Godot 4 with C#, with an engine-free simulation
library.

## Vision

- **Setting.** Starts 1 April 1996, right before the Big Three era. Additional
  start dates (e.g. autumn 1983, 2008) may be added later.
- **Research.** Ground relevant geography, costs, services and events in real
  Japanese data for Tokyo, with dates appropriate to the setting. Distinguish
  historical evidence, modern comparisons and deliberate gameplay estimates.
- **World.** Fake-but-close names for manga, magazines, publishers and people
  (Two Piece, Blanch, DraggingBall, Weekly Shonen Leap, Akiro Tokiyama). History
  runs on rails: scripted rivals launch on schedule and reach their real-timeline
  outcomes. If the player beats one, the rival still "wins" its scripted place;
  the player's manga simply becomes more successful.
- **Goals.** Endless play. Cancellation is a setback, not a loss. Milestones:
  serialization, anime adaptation, Tezuka-style awards.
- **Continuity and money.** If the starting prodigy leaves a business, its
  studios continue independently and the player follows the mangaka. Their
  lead series follow them going forward, without rewriting past rights.
  Personal money and business funds are separate; studio purchases use the
  business balance. A start-of-game difficulty option selects whether other
  departing leads retain their titles automatically or require player release.
  When joining an existing employer, the player manages the mangaka's team
  and series within an agreed budget; the employer runs the wider business.
  Voluntary personal investment and era-appropriate personal borrowing can
  fund an early studio. Incorporation, later financing and career/title
  transitions are implemented in Sub-project 3.
- **Staff.** Needs limited to hunger, thirst, comfort, and happiness (pay and
  atmosphere). No wider life sim.
- **Locations.** The same rent-free parents' house in a random outer Tokyo ward, followed
  by affordable, predetermined property tiers with multiple tradeoffs per tier.
  Larger operations can open additional studios. Local convention travel uses
  a Greater Tokyo metropolitan map; exact boundaries and expansion rules are
  implemented in Sub-project 3.
- **Tone.** Between Football Manager and Kairosoft.
- **Presentation.** Management UI with charts, plus a real-time 3D office in the
  relevant screens. In-game manga is shown in abridged form to stay clear of
  copyright.
- **Time.** Real time with pause. One tick is one game hour. One game day per
  real minute at 1x. Fixed speeds only (pause, 1x, 2x, 4x, 8x); no uncapped
  fast-forward, because the rhythm of the game is daily. When the last worker
  leaves, the game jumps to the next arrival and shows a daily recap.
- **Release.** Intended for eventual commercial release, once the owner is
  happy with it.

## Stack

- Godot 4.x, C# (.NET).
- `MangakaSim`: engine-free C# class library holding all simulation logic.
- `MangakaSim.Tests`: xUnit tests, including scenario/balance tests.
- Godot project: rendering, UI, real time, input, speed control, idle skip.
- Godot owns real time. The sim only ever sees whole hour ticks via
  `Advance(hours)`, which keeps it deterministic and headless-testable.
