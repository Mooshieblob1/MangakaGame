# Mangaka Studio: Roadmap

Working title: **Mangaka Studio**. A real-time management sim in the vein of
Software Inc., but for manga. The player is an omniscient director of one
mangaka who grows into a studio. Staff work semi-autonomously toward assigned
series. Built in Godot 4 with C#, with an engine-free simulation library.

## Vision

- **Setting.** Starts 1 April 1996, right before the Big Three era. Additional
  start dates (e.g. autumn 1983, 2008) may be added later.
- **World.** Fake-but-close names for manga, magazines, publishers and people
  (Two Piece, Blanch, DraggingBall, Weekly Shonen Leap, Akiro Tokiyama). History
  runs on rails: scripted rivals launch on schedule and reach their real-timeline
  outcomes. If the player beats one, the rival still "wins" its scripted place;
  the player's manga simply becomes more successful.
- **Goals.** Endless play. Cancellation is a setback, not a loss. Milestones:
  serialization, anime adaptation, Tezuka-style awards.
- **Staff.** Needs limited to hunger, thirst, comfort, and happiness (pay and
  atmosphere). No wider life sim.
- **Tone.** Between Football Manager and Kairosoft.
- **Presentation.** Management UI with charts, plus a real-time 3D office in the
  relevant screens. In-game manga is shown in abridged form to stay clear of
  copyright.
- **Time.** Real time with pause. One tick is one game hour. One game day per
  real minute at 1x. Fixed speeds only (pause, 1x, 2x, 4x, 8x); no uncapped
  fast-forward, because the rhythm of the game is daily. When the last worker
  leaves, the game jumps to the next arrival and shows a daily recap.
- **Release.** Intended for eventual commercial release, once the owner is
  happy with it.

## Stack

- Godot 4.x, C# (.NET).
- `MangakaSim`: engine-free C# class library holding all simulation logic.
- `MangakaSim.Tests`: xUnit tests, including scenario/balance tests.
- Godot project: rendering, UI, real time, input, speed control, idle skip.
- Godot owns real time. The sim only ever sees whole hour ticks via
  `Advance(hours)`, which keeps it deterministic and headless-testable.

## Sub-projects

Each sub-project gets its own spec, implementation plan, and build before the
next begins. Ideas raised during brainstorming are listed under the sub-project
that owns them so they are not lost.

### 1. Simulation core
Status: complete on 2026-09-22. See [completion notes](../sub-project-1-completion.md).

Spec: `2026-09-22-sim-core-design.md`.
Clock, one mangaka, one series, staged chapter pipeline (Name, Pencils, Inks,
Backgrounds, Tones), planner with player overrides, due dates with tracking
only, daily recap, save/load, deterministic ticks, debug UI in Godot.

### 2. Publishing and market
Status: complete on 2026-09-22. See [completion notes](../sub-project-2-completion.md).

Spec: `2026-09-22-publishing-market-design.md`. Plan: `../plans/2026-09-22-publishing-market.md`.

- Publishers and magazines (multiple, switchable), pitching, serialization
  contracts, editor check stage inserted into the chapter pipeline.
- Weekly reader rankings, volume (tankobon) sales, cancellation rules, income.
- Chapter and series **quality** is introduced here (skill, fatigue, time
  pressure, skipped stages) so rankings have something to judge.
- Late-chapter history from sub-project 1 gets consequences here.
- **Doujin**: series with no publisher, sold through an alternative channel.
- **Product formats** on top of the same chapters: tankobon first, then
  omnibus, deluxe, and bunko reissues for long-running or finished series.
- Market is **Japan only** in the base model. An "international interest" stat
  per series exists from here but stays at zero until the timeline enables it.

### 3. Staff and studio
- Hiring pool, skills per stage, needs (hunger, thirst, comfort), happiness
  (pay, atmosphere), pay, task assignment by skill and player choice.
- Overtime starts costing needs and happiness here.
- **Moonlighting** as a happiness/loyalty mechanic for staff.
- Assistants with near-real names (Akiro Tokiyama, Eiichido Oga). Plan for
  famous-analogue assistants to appear at their historical moments; the
  schedule is supplied by sub-project 5.

### 4. 3D office
Godot scene driven by the sim: desks, kitchen, toilet, characters walking and
working. Readable at 1x to 8x.

### 5. Historical timeline and rivals
- Scripted fake manga launching on schedule with on-rails outcomes.
- Era events: anime boom and overseas licensing (early 2000s), scanlation and
  piracy sites (mid 2000s) eating overseas sales, official digital simulpub
  (late 2010s) shrinking that effect, streaming-driven demand (2010s).
- Player levers such as pushing a publisher toward official digital release.
- Scheduled appearances of famous-analogue assistants (feeds sub-project 3).
- Additional start dates, if the game earns them.

### 6. Management UI and charts
Full screens, graphs, rankings, and the abridged manga viewer.

### 7. Awards, anime adaptation, milestones, balance, polish
Tezuka-style awards, anime adaptation, difficulty settings (scaling starting
skills), balance passes, polish.
