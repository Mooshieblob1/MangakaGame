Status: decision record supporting the [formal design draft](2026-09-23-staff-studio-design.md).
Confirmed choices below remain requirements; discussion recommendations are

## Confirmed choices

- Ground relevant mechanics and content in real Japanese data for Tokyo,
  respecting the 1996 setting. Distinguish historical facts, modern comparison
  points and game balance; see the [research ledger](2026-09-23-tokyo-research.md).
- The initial house is the mangaka's parents' home or equivalent family home,
  with zero rent.
- The player sets teams and priorities. Staff select tasks automatically,
  with manual assignment overrides available.
- Each series has a main team. Available staff can help other teams when free.
- Overtime is automatic when deadlines are at risk, within limits set by the
  player, with warnings about staff wellbeing and extra pay.
- Moonlighting is controlled through a studio policy with individual
  exceptions. Allowing outside creative work supports happiness and loyalty,
  but can reduce recovery time.
- Skills improve through practice and mentoring by stronger colleagues.
- Hired staff can lead their own studio series. The player can hire
  experienced leads or develop assistants into leads, supported by teams.
- The lead personally handles Name (story and rough page layouts). Pencils,
  inks, backgrounds and tones can be delegated to assistants.
- Title ownership is a difficulty option selected at the start. One mode
  lets all departing lead mangaka retain their titles going forward. The
  other keeps hired leads' series at the studio by default, with a player
  option to let the creator take them. The starting prodigy retains their
  lead titles going forward in both modes.
- If a series transfers to its departing creator, the studio retains income
  from books already published; future books belong to the creator.
- Unpaid wages and bills become arrears, with warnings, declining happiness
  and eventual departures. The player can downsize and recover; there is no
  game over and no automatic emergency loan under this choice.
- The player chooses facilities and a provision budget. Supplies and staff
  breaks are handled automatically.
- Leads can start the next chapter while assistants finish earlier artwork,
  with a small limit on chapters in progress.
- Hiring combines a random candidate pool and paid recruitment. Recruitment
  is a better route to strong talent; the pool still has a chance of a gem.
- Special situations can introduce optional hires. Include a reasonably
  rare employee-family introduction such as an employee's uncle asking to
  join, producing a guaranteed player-like prodigy if hired. The rare roll
  determines whether this event occurs, not a second chance to be a prodigy.
- The family-introduction prodigy's talent is revealed after they start
  working, rather than disclosed before hiring.
- Prodigies have high starting skills and faster learning, with the same
  maximum skill level as ordinary staff. They also have an increased chance
  to create an iconic series, beyond their starting-skill and learning bonuses.
- The extra iconic potential takes the form of occasional creative
  breakthroughs: standout chapters or arcs boost cultural impact when
  published successfully.
- Doujin printing supports both budgeted automation and manual print orders,
  with the player able to switch modes and optimise costs manually. Provide
  price tiers, including a basic convenience-store printing option such as
  the fictional "7-Twelve" for starting out.
- Physical printing quality affects sales only marginally for new or small
  doujin series. Its importance grows as a series becomes established: cheap
  convenience-store printing should matter much more for a long-running,
  well-known series, such as the user's 500-chapter example.
- Conventions use assigned staff, taking them away from production. Events
  include both free and paid attendance, with distance-based travel from the
  player's studio on a Greater Tokyo metropolitan-area map. Keep travel local
  between wards and surrounding areas; exclude distant intercity trips such
  as Tokyo to Osaka. Free conventions waive the attendance fee, but staff
  time and applicable travel costs remain. Travel is also free when a venue
  is close enough to walk or cycle to; this does not waive attendance time.
- Everyone starts with the same starter house in a randomly chosen outer
  ward of Tokyo; the player does not select the starting location. Later
  moves offer predetermined, tiered properties, with many alternatives per
  tier and varying benefits and downsides.
- Higher property tiers are accessible through affordability alone, without
  an additional reputation or milestone requirement. A sufficiently large
  operation can also open additional studios at other locations; the size
  condition for this expansion remains to be defined.
- The starting prodigy mangaka is the continuing focus of the game and its
  events. The player does not necessarily play as that person, but if they
  leave a studio, the player's perspective follows them rather than staying
  with the old studio.
- If the starting prodigy leaves the business entirely, the old studio and
  its branches continue independently. The player follows the prodigy's next
  venture and no longer manages the previous business remotely.
- Series and titles led by the departing starting prodigy stay with that
  mangaka going forward, without retroactively changing prior rights or
  transactions. Other leads follow the ownership difficulty option chosen
  at the start.
- The mangaka has personal money, separate from business funds. Studio
  purchases and operating expenses use the business balance. Personal funds
  follow the mangaka; business funds are not the same wallet.
- Manga revenue goes to the business, which pays creators a salary and a
  royalty share into their personal funds. Exact rates and payment timing
  remain to be specified.
- Only the player decides whether the starting prodigy leaves a studio.
  Events can present reasons or opportunities to leave but cannot force the
  move. Other employees' dissatisfaction-driven departures remain in scope.
- All locations of one business share its balance, with income and costs
  broken down per location rather than separately funded branch wallets.
- When the starting mangaka joins an existing employer, the player manages
  their team and series within an agreed employer budget. The employer runs
  the wider business; joining does not grant control of all its operations.
- Both the player and staff can propose series. The player can create
  projects, and aspiring leads can pitch ideas that require player approval.
- Studio properties are rented, with an upfront deposit and recurring rent.
  Outright property purchase is outside this milestone's selected approach.
- The player may voluntarily transfer personal savings into their own
  business to start it, expand it or cover a shortfall. Personal funds are
  never taken automatically to rescue the business.
- Add personal borrowing before incorporation, including loans and credit
  cards appropriate to Japan and the game's era. These are player-selected
  borrowing options, not the previously rejected automatic emergency loan.
  Incorporation is a player decision, with a setup cost and ongoing
  administration costs, opening access to business financing. Exact costs
  and subsequent financing rules remain to be designed.
- After incorporation, business loans become the main funding route, while
  personal borrowing and voluntary investment remain available. Existing
  personal debts still require repayment.
- Staff marketing supports both ongoing promotion and short campaigns for
  new releases, using staff time and a budget set by the player.
- Sub-project 3 requires a new game. Existing saves are not migrated into
  the new ownership, finance, staff and location systems.

## Intended experience

Grow from one creator into a studio where the player chooses people, work,
pay and working conditions. Staff should handle ordinary task selection and
breaks themselves. The interesting decisions are whether a hire removes a
bottleneck, whether the studio can afford them, and how hard to push a team
when a deadline is approaching.

Confirmed perspective: the starting prodigy is the continuing narrative focus,
even when the business grows across several locations. The player is not
necessarily roleplaying that person directly, but events are centred on them
and the player's perspective follows them if they leave a studio. The game
must not remain permanently attached to the original business or building.
Other staff and hiring events remain part of the agreed design.

Distinguish a physical office/location from the studio business that operates
it. Moving between branches and leaving the business need explicit, separate
rules. Confirmed: if the prodigy leaves the business entirely, its studio and
branches continue independently; the player follows the prodigy's next venture
and does not retain remote management of the previous business.

Confirmed: the departing prodigy retains the series and titles they led going
forward. Personal money follows the mangaka and is separate from business funds.
Only the player decides to leave; events can offer reasons or opportunities,
but unhappiness or another autonomous event cannot force this transition.
Still define departure opportunities, what staff and obligations follow, and
how the next business is funded. Do not silently transfer the old business's
assets or erase arrears when perspective changes. Define a bounded model
for the independent business's continued production, employment and finances,
including its effect on the market, rather than implying it disappears when
the player leaves. Simulation detail and presentation remain design work.

The roadmap limits needs to hunger, thirst, comfort and happiness. Keep that
boundary: no housing, relationships or wider life simulation. The engine
remains deterministic, with whole-hour ticks and the existing fixed speeds.

## Who creates the series?

Confirmed: hired staff can lead their own studio series. The player can hire
experienced leads or develop assistants into leads, each supported by a team.
Aki is the founding creator. Recommendation: represent being a lead as a
series assignment rather than a permanent employee class.

Confirmed: the player can create series, and staff can pitch their own ideas
for player approval. Staff proposals do not automatically start production
or commit the business's money. Define proposal frequency, contents, expiry
and how approval assigns a lead, team and priority. This internal studio pitch
is distinct from the existing one-shot pitch to a publisher. Approval while
working for another employer must also respect the agreed team budget.

Confirmed: the lead personally handles Name (story and rough page layouts).
Pencils, inks, backgrounds and tones can be delegated to assistants.

Confirmed: title ownership is a difficulty option selected when starting a
game, with both of the following modes supported:

- Creator retention: every departing lead mangaka retains their lead titles
  going forward, including hired staff and the starting prodigy.
- Studio retention for hired leads: their series stays with the studio by
  default and the player can appoint a replacement lead, but the player may
  choose to let the departing creator take it. The starting prodigy's lead
  titles still follow them going forward when they leave.

These descriptive mode names are provisional. Preserve the choice in the
save and apply it consistently to later departures; do not infer permission
for a mid-game switch. Authorship, past contributions and prior publication
rights remain recorded in both modes. The voluntary transfer option remains
part of the studio-retention mode, not a future contract-negotiation feature.

Working interpretation of "not retroactively": preserve existing publication
rights, income already earned and historical authorship; new work follows the
departing creator. This is consistent with the previously agreed treatment
of published books. Define the effective departure boundary, including
unreleased books, work in progress and a former lead later replaced by another.

Confirmed income rule: the studio retains income from books already
published when the series transfers. Future books belong to the creator.
Still define treatment of unsold doujin stock, scheduled but unreleased books,
unfinished chapters, publisher contracts and future outside production.
Full ownership negotiation at hiring is outside the current confirmed scope.

## Departure destinations and colleagues

The user identified three possible destinations for the starting mangaka:
joining a new employer, forming their own studio, or returning home to make
doujin. Following colleagues must fit the destination; acceptance by a new
employer cannot be assumed. The following is a proposed approach awaiting
the user's response, not a confirmed blanket right to take employees.

- Existing employer: the player can request places for selected colleagues
  as part of the move. The employer decides whom it can accept and on what
  terms. Colleagues separately decide whether they want those offers.
- New independent studio: invite selected colleagues subject to their
  willingness, actual workspace capacity and affordable employment offers.
  Define how personal funds seed the new business before making commitments.
- Home-based doujin: offer the same invitation choice, but with the starter
  house's smaller capacity and budget. Propose allowing the mangaka to return
  alone and hire help later. Loyalty should not silently turn staff into
  unpaid permanent assistants.

Recommendation: willingness depends on loyalty to the mangaka, happiness at
the old business and the proposed role/pay. Show the destination, offers,
capacity and expected payroll before committing the move. A colleague should
not resign merely because the player requested an employer interview or
explored a destination. Confirm employer acceptance and the colleague's
acceptance before scheduling their transfer. Staff who do not follow remain
with the independent old business, subject to its ordinary employment rules.

Confirmed player control when joining an existing employer: management of
the mangaka's team and series within an agreed employer budget. The employer
runs the wider business. Joining as a creator does not grant access to its
entire balance or control of unrelated teams and branches. Define the budget
agreement, permitted spending, staffing approvals and how to request changes.
Returning-home availability, startup funding, moving dates and notice remain
to be specified. All routes preserve the chosen ownership difficulty rule
and historical income rights.

## Production needs a team pipeline

The current planner assigns every stage to Aki and creates the next chapter
only after the current one finishes. Merely adding employees would leave
most of a team idle behind the five sequential stages.

Confirmed: leads can start the next chapter while assistants finish earlier
artwork, with a small limit on chapters in progress. Recommendation: unlock
the next Name once the preceding chapter's Name is accepted (or completed
for doujin). Start with one worker per stage; different chapters and series
can progress at the same time. Same-stage collaboration can remain a later
refinement. The exact chapter limit and unlock rule remain to be specified.

The design must fix the pipeline limit, publication-slot allocation and
buffer target. Keep pitches to a single one-shot. Never generate several
chapters for one issue, publish chapters out of order, or let an idle team
create an unlimited production buffer.

Confirmed assignment model: the player sets teams and priorities, staff select
tasks automatically, and manual overrides are available. Each series has a
main team, with free staff able to help other teams. Automatic assignment
should consider stage skill, availability and existing commitments. The
design still needs to define when a worker counts as free and how borrowed
staff return to their main team's work without disruptive task switching.
Avoid constant reassignment of partly completed work. An unavailable expert
should not silently block a deadline when an eligible substitute exists;
manual locks need a visible warning when they prevent useful work.

Work for an hour must be chosen before applying that hour's completions.
Otherwise the order of people in the save can let one assistant begin the
next stage in the same hour that another person finishes its prerequisite.
Deadline forecasts must account for dependencies, shared workers, schedules,
breaks and editor waits rather than add up nominal staff hours.

## Hiring, growth and departures

Confirmed: provide both a random candidate pool and paid recruitment.
Recruitment offers better odds of strong talent, while the ordinary pool
can still contain an exceptional gem. Recommendation: recruitment can target
a particular stage specialty, with a cost and delay. Exact odds, costs,
search duration and information visible before hiring remain to be specified.
Do not turn paid recruitment into a guarantee of a prodigy.

Confirmed: special situations can introduce optional hires. Include a rare
employee-family introduction, for example: "An employee's uncle wants to
work here. Can he?" The intended interpretation is that this event's candidate
is guaranteed to be a player-like prodigy if hired; the event itself has a
reasonably low chance of occurring. Other hiring events need not guarantee
prodigies. Confirmed: the family-introduction prodigy's exceptional talent is
revealed after they start working, making the introduction a surprise.
The reveal timing and presentation still need to be defined. Avoid disclosing
this candidate's exceptional stats through a pre-hire detail panel.

Define eligibility, offer expiry, event frequency and repeat limits so a
larger staff cannot flood the studio with prodigies. The player chooses whether to hire;
an introduction must not silently add an employee or payroll obligation.

Use the saved deterministic RNG for candidates, recruitment and hiring
events. Define pool refresh and expiry, and save pending introductions and
resolved outcomes so reloading cannot reroll them. These are studio-life
events in Sub-project 3. Historically scheduled famous analogues remain in
Sub-project 5.

Confirmed progression: skills improve through practice and mentoring by
stronger colleagues. Define mentoring eligibility, the mentor's time cost,
learning rates and caps in the design. Recommendation: practice improves
the stage actually worked. Paid courses, elaborate personality systems and
poaching negotiations remain outside the confirmed scope.

Confirmed prodigy model: high starting skills, faster learning and the same
maximum skill level as everyone else. Ordinary staff retain access to that
ceiling through development. Prodigies also have an increased chance of
creating an iconic series; this is an additional creative advantage, not
merely a restatement of their higher starting skills. Apply the same trait
definition to the starting mangaka and other prodigies, including the rare
family-introduction candidate. Exact starting skill ranges and learning
rates remain balance work.

Current code awards iconic status when cultural impact reaches 90 and the
fanbase reaches 1,000,000; there is no standalone iconic-chance roll. The
design must explicitly connect the new creative advantage to this system.
Confirmed mechanism: occasional creative breakthroughs, represented by
standout chapters or arcs that boost cultural impact when published
successfully. A steady passive impact-growth bonus is not the selected
approach. Recommended scope: apply the advantage to work a prodigy leads,
preserving ordinary creators' path to iconic status and existing frozen
chapter quality. The breakthrough frequency, magnitude, meaning of successful
publication and chapter-versus-arc representation remain design work.

Recommendation: determine and save a breakthrough with its creative work,
then award its impact once the publication condition is met. Define a doujin
release equivalent so the feature is not silently limited to serialized work.
Freeze relevant lead attribution so a last-minute reassignment cannot grant
the bonus to old work, and avoid stacking the same bonus for every prodigy
assistant. Save the outcome so reloading cannot reroll it or award it twice.

Staff should give understandable dissatisfaction warnings before resigning.
Define notice, dismissal, accrued pay and work reassignment. Retain departed
people as historical records: chapters, reputation awards and events already
refer to contributor IDs. Only current staff should contribute to the
studio's active staff-reputation term. The starting prodigy can leave a studio
only by player choice, and the player's perspective follows them. Their
unhappiness can inform decisions but cannot force a departure; define the
transition rules separately from other employees' resignation rules.

## Needs, overtime and quality

Confirmed: the player chooses facilities and a provision budget, while
supplies and staff breaks are handled automatically. Still define budget
levels, supply costs and the behaviour when provision is underfunded.

Treat hunger, thirst and comfort as short-term needs, with automatic breaks
and recovery away from work. Happiness reflects sustained pay, atmosphere
and overtime conditions. Show why a person is unhappy and what would help.

Use abstract facilities and access/capacity rules now: workstations, food,
drinks and comfort provision. Leave furniture placement, walking and physical
queues to Sub-project 4. The same simulation activities should later drive
the office scene. With one-hour ticks, a separate hour for every drink or
comfort visit would consume too much of the day; define combined breaks or
coarser need thresholds deliberately.

Confirmed: overtime is automatic when deadlines are at risk, within
player-set limits, with wellbeing and extra-pay warnings. The exact limits,
compensation and warning thresholds remain to be specified.

Overtime should solve occasional emergencies but worsen needs and sustained
happiness when repeated. Avoid adding a broad fatigue system as another
unrelated life-sim meter. If tiredness affects quality, derive it from an
explicit workload/recovery rule and explain its relationship to the existing
overtime quality penalty. Do not accidentally charge the same penalty twice.

Replace the current last-worker quality rule when introducing handovers.
Recommendation: accumulate quality using each worker's effective skill when
work is performed, weighted by useful work contributed, then freeze it on
completion. A high-skill worker doing the final hour must not upgrade an
entire stage. Specify editor redo handling and preserve historical quality
when workers improve, transfer or leave.

This supports several simultaneous locations, not just replacement moves.
The meaning of large enough, location limits and staffing/management rules

Suggested tradeoffs include price, staff capacity, amenity space, atmosphere
and proximity to conventions. These specific attributes are proposals, not
all separately confirmed. Confirmed acquisition model: studio properties
are rented with an upfront deposit and recurring rent; outright purchase is
not selected for this milestone. Define the number of tiers and properties,
deposit amounts/refunds, rent cadence, moving costs and any production
disruption in the design. Downsizing and recovery must remain compatible with
the agreed arrears system. Whether the starter house remains available as a
fallback is still open.

Multiple locations require explicit staff bases, team allocation, local
capacity and provision, operating bills and convention travel origins. Define
whether helping another location requires travel and how a player monitors
branches while the main perspective follows the starting prodigy. Keep the

Represent property identity, location, capacity and operating effects in the

## Pay, bills and recovery

Sub-project 2 provides income and a ledger, but no recurring expenses. The
next design needs salaries, overtime compensation policy, payment cadence,
partial employment periods and a clear view of upcoming commitments.

Confirmed: separate the mangaka's personal money from business funds. Studio
purchases and operating expenses are charged to the business. Changing the
player's perspective does not merge or transfer the former business's wallet
into the mangaka's personal funds. These balances need separate ownership and
transaction histories.

Confirmed: all locations belonging to one business share one balance. Show
costs and earnings per location for management visibility, without requiring
manual transfers or creating independent branch budgets. Separate businesses
retain separate funds even if the prodigy previously worked at both.

In the confirmed existing-employer route, the player's agreed team budget is
a spending allowance within that employer's business funds. Recommendation:
account for it as authorised spending rather than minting a second copy of
cash. Exact allowance renewal, remaining commitments and overspend handling
are still design work. The shared-balance choice does not grant the player
unrestricted access to an employer's entire business balance.

Confirmed income routing: manga revenue goes to the business, which pays
creators a salary and a royalty share into their personal funds. Still define
the royalty basis and rates, payment timing, creator attribution for existing
books and lead handovers, and handling of unpaid creator compensation.
Studio retention of existing-book income means the old business remains the
revenue recipient; creator entitlements need explicit rules rather than being
silently extinguished on departure.

Confirmed: voluntary transfers from personal savings can fund the player's
own business at startup or later for expansion or a shortfall. Do not require
an automatic bailout. The accounting treatment of these contributions,
withdrawals and starting balances remains to be defined. The user's "separate currency"
requires separate spendable balances; denomination or conversion mechanics
have not been chosen. Do not invent an exchange rate or spend personal money
automatically when the business cannot pay a bill.

The current single-balance implementation starts at 500,000 yen; how that
starting amount maps to the two new balances remains open. Choose game balance values after
checking the time to first income; do not present proposed wages as historical
facts. Make a solo start viable and an early assistant a meaningful expense.
Use the existing price index consistently, distinguishing agreed salary from
changing market expectations and operating prices.

Mandatory bills cannot use the current purchase rule, which rejects debits
larger than cash. Confirmed: unpaid wages and bills become arrears, with
warnings, declining happiness and eventual departures. The player can
downsize and recover without a game over; an automatic emergency loan is not
the selected approach. Still define discretionary spending restrictions,
repayment order, partial payments and a viable return to a minimal solo
studio. Reducing staff must not erase owed pay.

## Personal borrowing and incorporation

Confirmed: allow player-selected personal loans and credit-card borrowing
before incorporation, using options appropriate to Japan and the 1996 start.
These are deliberate funding choices, not automatic responses to unpaid
bills. Confirmed: incorporation is a player decision rather than an automatic
size or income milestone. It has a setup cost and ongoing administration
costs and opens access to business financing. Define the exact costs, timing
and financing terms in the design; availability of a loan product should not
silently imply automatic loan approval. Confirmed: business loans become the
main funding route after incorporation, while personal borrowing and voluntary
investment remain available. Existing personal debts still require repayment.

Research checked on 2026-09-23 supports period-appropriate categories:

- [SMBC Consumer Finance's history](https://www.smbc-cf.com/english/corporate/history.html)
  records Promise consumer lending from 1963 and Tokyo expansion in 1969.
  A fictional consumer-finance personal lender fits the starting era.
- [JACCS's history](https://www.jaccs.co.jp/en/corporate/about/history/)
  records credit cards from 1970, bank card-loan guarantees in 1982 and a
  card-cash-loan rate change in February 1997. Cards and cash lending fit the
  period; do not backdate that particular 1997 rate to April 1996.
- [JFC's institutional history](https://www.jfc.go.jp/n/english/report/pdf/jfc2025e_5.pdf)
  identifies the People's Finance Corporation from 1949, its 1999 name change
  and JFC's creation in 2008. If adding a public small-business lender, use
  era-appropriate fictional naming rather than placing present-day JFC in 1996.

These sources establish historical existence, not a ranking of the most
common borrowing channels for mangaka or eligibility for a particular loan.
Current loan offers and legal limits must not be copied into the 1996 balance
model. Exact historical conditions require further research if represented;
otherwise label amounts and rates as fictional game balance parameters.

Recommended bounded game model, pending detailed design:

- A credit-card cash-advance facility for small, flexible personal borrowing,
  and a personal loan with a defined principal and repayment schedule. Keep
  purchase credit distinct from withdrawing cash; do not let a shopping limit
  become free cash for wages or transfers.
- Show the interest rate, available limit, repayment schedule and outstanding
  debt before accepting. Approval and limits should depend on income, existing
  obligations and repayment history, not grant unlimited funding to a prodigy.
- Borrowed personal cash enters the personal balance; a separate voluntary
  transfer funds the business. Record the corresponding personal debt, principal
  repayments and interest without treating borrowing as earned manga revenue.
- Outstanding personal debt remains attached to the mangaka on departure or
  incorporation. Incorporation must not delete it, refill exhausted credit
  limits or silently reassign it to the company. Any refinancing requires
  explicit rules. New personal borrowing remains available after incorporation
  as confirmed; define which business loan products become available.
- Define missed-payment consequences and a viable recovery path consistent
  with endless play. Keep ordinary business arrears distinct from accepted
  loan obligations, and test both balances together across studio transitions.

## Costs and marketing carried over from Sub-project 2

The approved publishing design assigns printing costs, print runs, convention
travel, provider fees and staff marketing to this milestone. They are part
of its scope discussion even though the roadmap's short staff outline does
not repeat them. Any deferral needs an explicit update to the design.

Confirmed printing direction:

- Support both automatic print runs within a player-set budget and manual
  ordering for players who want to optimise costs. Modes must be switchable;
  avoid duplicate orders when switching or manually supplementing automation.
- Offer price tiers, including basic small-run convenience-store printing
  such as fictional "7-Twelve". The user's inkjet example establishes the
  inexpensive entry-level flavour, not a requirement to simulate printer
  hardware or make a historical claim about real shops.
- Recommended starting tiers: convenience-store copies with low upfront
  commitment and higher per-copy cost; a local print shop for medium batches;
  professional bulk printing with setup costs and cheaper large runs.
  Exact prices, minimum batches and production delays remain design work.
- Show total cost, cost per copy, quantity and delivery time before ordering.
  Manual cost optimisation should balance batch discounts against tied-up
  cash and unsold stock. Define automation's demand estimate, reorder point,
  spending period and pending-order accounting.
- Confirmed: physical printing quality affects sales, but its initial effect
  is very small. New or small doujin series remain viable with basic copies.
  As a series becomes established, readers expect better presentation, and
  poor printing has a much stronger effect. A 500-chapter series still using
  basic convenience-store copies is the user's example, not an exact cutoff.
- Recommendation: scale reader expectations gradually using released work
  and audience recognition. Do not count unpublished production buffers as
  establishment or apply a major penalty solely because a series first gains
  serialization. A long-running, established doujin can also develop higher
  expectations. Exact inputs, caps and sales effects remain to be specified.
- Keep physical print quality separate from the manga's frozen chapter
  quality. Better paper must not upgrade the story/art or rewrite past reader
  rankings. Whether the player can adjust selling prices remains open.

Remaining recommended treatment:

- Doujin print runs have an upfront cost, available stock and a reorder
  decision; sales cannot exceed stock. Publisher-funded commercial printing
  continues through the existing royalty arrangement unless explicitly changed.
- Confirmed convention direction: assign staff to attend and sell books,
  taking them away from production in exchange for sales and new fans. Some
  conventions have an attendance fee and some are free. Base travel on the
  distance from the studio to the venue within a Greater Tokyo metropolitan
  map, including local travel between wards; do not introduce distant trips
  such as Tokyo to Osaka.
- Confirmed free-convention rule: free means zero attendance fee; staff time
  and applicable travel costs still apply. Nearby venues within walking or
  cycling distance have zero travel charge, whether the convention itself
  is free or paid. Free travel does not imply zero travel or attendance time.
  Define the walking/cycling distance threshold and whole-hour time treatment
  in the design; neither an exact radius nor a transport-speed value has
  been chosen yet.
- Studio location follows the confirmed starter-house and tiered-relocation
  model above. Still define the map boundary and locations, relocation details,
  travel-time/cost rules, event calendars, attendance capacity, stock carried,
  bookings and cancellations. Start with simulation locations and a readable
  event list; map presentation must be scoped separately from the later 3D
  office. Exact geography and venue references should be verified when chosen.
- Going online retains its setup purchase and gains a recurring provider
  bill. Define service suspension, reconnection and effects on existing books
  when a bill is unpaid.
- Confirmed: staff marketing supports both ongoing promotion and short
  campaigns for new releases, using staff time and a player-set budget.
  It competes for the same person's time as production and needs bounded,
  visible reach effects. Define its skill basis, duration, costs, campaign
  overlap and staffing so it cannot be free passive growth or repeatedly
  stacked without limit. Define which channels require internet access and
  avoid introducing modern promotional platforms into the 1996 start.

These additions change the early doujin route. Test cash, stock and sales
together, including a failed first print run and recovery without royalties.

## Moonlighting

Keep this focused on happiness and loyalty as the roadmap requests.
Confirmed: a studio policy with individual exceptions governs employees'
outside creative work. Allowing it supports happiness and loyalty but can
reduce recovery time. The design should provide limited opportunities and
clear time/recovery implications. Specify whether outside earnings
belong solely to the employee; do not merge them into studio income by default.

An employee's outside project need not become another fully simulated series
in this milestone. Avoid hidden theft of studio work, sudden arbitrary
departures or a ban that is always optimal. Rival offers and historical
career moves belong to Sub-project 5.

## Delivery and evidence

The debug UI needs staff selection, candidates, pay, schedules, team and
stage assignments, need/happiness explanations, studio provision, operating
costs and moonlighting policy. Daily recaps and idle skipping must work with
staggered schedules and breaks. A break must not be mistaken for everyone
having left for the day.

Save validation currently requires exactly one person. A new format must
validate employment, historical contributors, assignments, obligations,
candidate state and production buffers. Confirmed: require a new game for
Sub-project 3; do not implement migration from version 2 or earlier formats.
Reject incompatible saves with a clear explanation before replacing the live
game. Keep the user's old save file intact. New-format saves must still
round-trip and resume deterministically.

Include the start-of-game ownership difficulty choice in the debug setup and
save format. Verify both modes, voluntary release in studio-retention mode,
the protagonist's departure under either mode, and unchanged historical
publication rights and income.

Acceptance scenarios should demonstrate useful assistance, competing series,
specialist handover without a quality exploit, reasonable needs recovery,
repeated overtime consequences, payroll shortfall and recovery, departure
without lost history, stock-limited sales and a worthwhile moonlighting
tradeoff. Require identical results for uninterrupted play, save/load and
command replay. Check actual Godot controls and rendered readability too.
