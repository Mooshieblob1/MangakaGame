# Music system: design

Date: 2026-09-28. Status: design approved in conversation (Q35, Q36 and three
design sections); written spec awaiting the user's review.

Background: Tier 2 lists music and sound. The Tier 2 answers decided
AI-generated music (Q1) in a modern lo-fi "study beats" style (Q2), generated by
the user in Suno from the prompts and 13-cue list in
[music-suno-prompts.md](../music-suno-prompts.md). The earlier plan to wire music
only after Tier 1 sign-off changed on 2026-09-28: the user chose to build the
music system while alpha.12 is with testers, because it does not touch what they
are judging.

## Goal

Music that suits what is happening, stays pleasant over long sessions (tester A
played 5.5 hours) and changes smoothly, with the game unchanged when no music
files exist yet. Music is presentation only: the simulation, its random numbers
and saves are untouched.

## Decisions

### Q35. How strongly the music reacts: gentle (decided 2026-09-28)

The time of day and the player's situation choose the next track. Only big
moments cut in with a short crossfade, play once, then hand back to the calm
rotation. Rejected: mood only (a cancellation could play over cheerful music)
and fully reactive (constant churn at 32x, tiring over long sessions).

### Q36. Music versus quiet: quiet stretches (decided 2026-09-28)

After each rotation track, 60 to 120 seconds of office ambience only. With four
daytime tracks of 2 to 3 minutes, back-to-back music would repeat every 10
minutes or so; the gaps roughly halve that and give the existing ambience a
role. Rejected: nearly continuous (repetition shows quickly) and mostly quiet
(3 to 5 minute gaps; the game may feel empty).

## What plays when

- **Main menu and its screens:** the title pool (`title-01`).
- **Calm rotation in a career:**
  - Daytime, 06:00 to 18:00 game time: the day pool.
  - Evening and night: the night pool.
  - Once the player's studio has moved out of the parents' house, the studio
    pool joins the day pool.
  - The time of day and situation are read only when the next track starts, so
    32x days (about 11 seconds each) and the overnight skip never cause
    switching.
  - The same track never plays twice in a row when the pool has another.
  - After each rotation track, a quiet gap of 60 to 120 seconds, chosen at
    random.
- **Big moments** crossfade in over about 3 seconds, play once, then the
  rotation resumes after a normal quiet gap:
  - good news (`good-news-`): a serialization offer, an accepted serialization,
    an award win (a prize), the career's first sale;
  - setback (`setback-`): a series cancellation, a rejected pitch;
  - convention (`convention-`): a convention day the player attends;
  - deadline (`deadline-`): a magazine chapter reported at risk.
  - If several happen together, the order is setback, good news, deadline,
    convention.
  - Each kind plays at most once per game day, and never during the overnight
    skip.
- **Pausing** the game does not stop music. The main menu switches to the title
  pool; returning to the career fades back into the rotation.
- **Window focus:** when the window loses focus, music fades out over about a
  second and pauses, then resumes when focus returns, matching the ambience.
- **Missing files:** a cue with no file stays quiet. With no music files at all,
  the game sounds exactly as it does today.

## Controls

- A "Music · 0 mutes" slider in Settings, beside the office ambience and sound
  effects sliders. Default 50%. Saved with the career in the presentation
  settings like the other two; older careers without the setting get the
  default. Values outside 0 to 1 are refused by the career validator, as for the
  other volumes.

## Files and adding tracks

- Tracks live in `godot/Assets/Music/`. The file name's prefix selects the pool:
  `title-`, `day-`, `night-`, `studio-`, `convention-`, `deadline-`,
  `good-news-`, `setback-`. Any number of files per pool; adding `day-05.ogg`
  extends the daytime rotation without code changes.
- Discovery scans the folder at start-up. In exported builds Godot lists
  imported files with `.import` or `.remap` suffixes; discovery strips those.
- Format in the game: OGG Vorbis. The user downloads WAV or MP3 from Suno;
  a conversion script (`scripts/convert-music.ps1`, using the installed ffmpeg)
  converts them to OGG and evens out loudness to one target level so no track
  jumps out. Only the OGG files are committed; the originals stay outside git.
- Provenance: the table in `music-suno-prompts.md` records each track's Suno
  plan, date and prompt. `private-alpha-credits.txt` gains a line that the
  music is AI-generated with Suno, which also serves the Steam AI-content
  disclosure.

## Structure

- **`MusicPlan`** (engine-free, `src/MangakaSim`, like `CareerGuidance`): holds
  the rules above. Inputs: the available tracks by pool, whether a menu is
  open, the game time, whether the studio has moved out, whether the overnight
  skip is running, new game events, and real seconds elapsed. Output: what the
  player should do now (start a track with a fade or a crossfade, keep playing,
  or stay quiet). It uses its own random generator, never the simulation's, so
  it cannot affect play or save replays; tests seed it.
- **`MusicPlayer`** (Godot node, `godot/Office/` beside `OfficeAudio`): two
  audio stream players for crossfades, file discovery, loading, volume and
  focus fades. It follows `MusicPlan` and reports when a track has finished.
- `DebugMain` creates the player, feeds it the events it already scans, and
  adds the slider.

## Error handling

- A file that fails to load is skipped for the session; its cue falls back to
  another track in the pool, or to quiet. The failure is written once to the
  session timeline, so a tester's report shows it. Music never stops the game.

## Testing

- **Automated tests for `MusicPlan`:** day and night pools; studio tracks
  joining after a move; no immediate repeats; quiet gaps between 60 and 120
  seconds; each big moment interrupting once and not twice in one game day;
  priority when several happen together; nothing during the overnight skip;
  no switching when 32x flips day and night within one track; title pool in
  menus; quiet for empty pools; an empty folder behaving like today.
- **Godot check:** a few seconds of generated tones stand in for tracks, to
  confirm crossfades start, volume 0 mutes, focus loss pauses, and the game
  runs with no music files.
- **Not verifiable by me:** whether the music sounds right in play. That needs
  the user's ears once real tracks exist, and then testers.

## Out of scope

A jukebox or track picker, per-track volume, lowering the ambience under music,
dynamic layering or stems, and music-driven gameplay.

## What the user does, and when

Only when that step arrives, as agreed: generate the tracks in Suno on a plan
with commercial rights, following `music-suno-prompts.md`, and save the
downloads where the conversion script can reach them. The system is built and
tested before any track exists.
