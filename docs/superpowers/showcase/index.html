構想Vision

The pitch

Software Inc., but for manga.

Mangaka Days sits between Football Manager and Kairosoft: decisions with real numbers behind them, presented through a tiny chibi world you want to keep watching. You direct the career of a prodigy mangaka, Aki by default, from a one-shot drawn at home to a serialized hit, a studio of assistants and an anime deal. The career follows the creator, not the company: change employers, found a studio or start again from home. Cancellation is a setback, never a game over, and the game never ends.

決断

Decisions with weight

What to draw, where to pitch, when to hire. Every choice shows its cost, its odds and what happens next.

愛嬌

Charm you can watch

An isometric 3D home and studio with chibi staff, family routines and light that follows the day.

東京

Grounded in real Tokyo

Clear progress, actionable notifications, and a companion who guides without ever playing for you.

Who it is for

  • Management and cosy-sim players who enjoy watching a small operation grow into a big one.
  • Manga readers curious about how chapters, surveys and cancellations really work.
  • Steam and Steam Deck players with 20 minutes or an evening to spare.

The promise in one line

Your first sale in 20 minutes. Your first studio in a few evenings. Your legacy over decades.

The name

Mangaka Days replaced the working title Mangaka Studio on 28 September 2026. The tagline on the title screen reads "A career told one page at a time." The logo, with Helper-Chan winking from its corner, sets the colours of the whole interface.

Logo and title art are AI-generated with NovelAI V5. Its terms allow store and title-screen use with Steam's AI disclosure. A commissioned designer brief covers the final logo, key art and Steam capsules.

相棒Companion

Mascot, guide and first reader

Helper-Chan never leaves your side.

A loyal assistant with enormous blonde drill twintails, glasses, a red lanyard and a clipboard she never puts down. She is the creator's number one fan. She teaches, notifies and occasionally steps into the story, and she follows every career move for free.

Her twintails are her silhouette, so on the 3D model they stay deliberately wider than anyone else's. In store art she always appears in her adult, fully clothed reference design.

The core career path

From the kitchen table to the reader survey.

One continuous path, taught step by step, runs from a blank career through a first hire and a first setback. Contests and employment sit beside it as side routes. Dates come from a guided playtest career on the Standard preset; real times are tester A's first career on alpha.12.

Time and pacing

One simulation tick is one in-game hour, played in real time. The working day runs 08:00 to 18:00, then a lights-out sequence carries the night to dawn.

32x runs quiet days without the recap and drops back to your last speed when something needs you. Tester A spent 39% of a 5.5 hour session at 32x and averaged about 2.6 real hours per in-game year, a third of it paused. On days with no work, the night is skipped too.

The editor's gate

Serialized chapters stop after Name for editor review: 24, 36 or 48 hours depending on the magazine's tier. Pencils wait until it clears. A redo costs a little reputation, and the third submission is always approved, so review is pressure rather than a wall.

Quality and shortcuts

A clean solo chapter at skill 95 scores about 96. Skipping Tones saves time and drops it to 86. Overtime rushes the work and trims quality, never below 70% of what it would have been. Skills grow with practice and mentoring.

Twelve genres, each with its own history

Genre demand follows keyframes from 1996 to 2020, with random booms and your own influence on top. Slice of life climbs from a niche to a staple by 2010; fantasy rises steadily; sci-fi cools. Every genre has original cover art for the showcase screens.

出版Publishing

Two ways to reach readers

Self-publish first. Then pitch for the magazines.

Doujin work is yours: no deadlines, no penalties, your own prices. Magazine work pays page fees and royalties but brings the reader survey, the editor and the risk of cancellation.

Doujin: print, list, sell

Online listings are free and the shop takes 30%. Copies sell through shop hours, 10:00 to 20:00 every day, so sales arrive as they happen, and internet reach grows year by year from almost nothing in 1996. Conventions come in three sizes: a free local event, a regional one in Toshima and the major summer and winter events at Ariake, with booths booked up to 28 days ahead.

Pitching

chance = base × quality × reputation × genre fit × trendBase odds are 12%, 24% and 40% for tiers 1, 2 and 3, held between 2% and 95%. A contest win adds 10 points for a year, and finishing the Rookie chapter adds 10 points to the next pitch.

A rejection names the weakest factor and closes that magazine to you for 26 weeks, and the Pitch button says so until then. An offer debuts at the fourth issue close, which is why the buffer matters.

The reader survey

rank score = 60% readership + 40% craftAdjusted by genre fit, trend and crowding from same-genre series.

New series are protected for six chapters. After that, weeks below the line bring a warning, then a cancellation roll. Long-running, popular and culturally important series earn more protection, and a truly iconic one can never be cancelled.

"Ink and Thunder" accepted at Monthly Hoshigaku Flowers: first issue 18 September 1996, ¥8,500 a page, chapters ready 0 of 2.

Collected volumes

Chapters gather into tankobon volumes six weeks after completion. Royalties follow the print run, as they do in Japan: the first 10,000 copies are paid on release, then each reprint milestone pays again. Digital and overseas editions open up as the timeline reaches them.

Endings

Cancellation closes that magazine for a year and dents your track record, but a final volume still comes out and you can pitch again elsewhere. Ending a series properly after twelve or more chapters improves your standing.

Separate pockets, clear consequences

Your savings are not the studio's money.

Personal savings and the doujin budget are always kept apart. Moving money between them is a deliberate act, and incorporation turns the budget into a proper company account. Figures below are the Standard preset.

仕事場Studio

The 3D office and the people in it

From the spare room to a floor in Minato.

An orthographic, dollhouse-style 3D view with roofs lifted and front walls cut away. You can rotate, pan and zoom, and switch to furnishing mode to place desks on a 0.25 m grid. Everyone is a chibi about two and a half heads tall.

The parents' house: Aki's desk heads a desk island, Helper-Chan's desk faces the spare one, and the old alcove is now a studio corner with a bookcase, plan chest, cork board, light table and screentone rack. Kitchen, genkan, WC and rear stairs lead to the family's upstairs.

The family

Mom and Dad live their own routines: they come down for meals, head out on errands and share breaks with you. They are pure atmosphere with no effect on the simulation, but they make the first months feel like home.

Hiring

A pool of six candidates refreshes twice a month: juniors, generalists, specialists and, very rarely, a prodigy. Salary expectations rise with skill, and a paid search finds three more. A new hire needs a free desk with a reachable chair, and assistants take Backgrounds and Tones unless you change it.

Wellbeing

Staff get hungry, thirsty and tired, take breaks, and slow down when they are running on empty. Unhappiness or unpaid wages lead to warnings and notice. Rival studios may try to poach your best people.

Growing

Move up through the desk tiers, open branches, take a job with another studio as an employed lead, or go home and start again. Old businesses go dormant, never deleted.

A desk that fits every screen.

The office fills the whole canvas. A persistent left rail opens floating panels for Goals, the Inbox, Series, Books, Staff, Finances, Studios and Industry. Built in from the start

  • Two themes from the logo. Evening studio, or Manuscript paper. Every text colour reaches 4.5:1 contrast.
  • Interface size from 80% to 200%. Automatic follows Windows scaling, capped so a 1280 x 720 layout still fits.
  • Fullscreen or windowed, switched with F11 or Alt+Enter.
  • Status never relies on colour alone, icons are always labelled, nothing essential hides behind hover.
  • Disabled controls say why. Visible focus and keyboard order throughout.
  • Reduced motion turns off fades, sparkles and bubbles; trails run Full, Reduced or Off.

Logo slabs and two palettes

Buttons are chunky slabs taken from the logo lettering: gold for the main action, mint for where you are, quiet slabs for everything else. Buttons and headings use Lilita One; body text and figures keep the reading font.

Moments, gently

A first sale, an offer, an award, a cancellation, a deadline at risk or a convention day crossfades to its own track over about three seconds, at most once a game day and never overnight or in menus. The clock is read only when a track starts, so 32x never makes the music jump.

Volume on every computer

Master, Music and Sound effects each have a slider, saved per computer rather than per career. Sound pauses when the window loses focus unless "Play sound even while unfocused" is ticked. Music uses its own random numbers, so it never changes a career.

Music is AI-generated with Suno on the Pro plan, which grants commercial use; provenance is recorded and will be disclosed on Steam. Tracks are swappable.

技術Technology

How it is built

A deterministic simulation under a charming skin.

The whole game world lives in an engine-free C# library. Godot owns real time and presentation; the simulation only ever sees whole-hour ticks and player commands, so the same seed and the same choices always produce the same career.

790automated simulation tests passing
40 yrsof continuous play tested through save and load
v10save format, importing every version since v3
~8 msper frame with 32 staff at 8x on a desktop GPU
  • Saves you can trust. Named snapshots, three rotating daily autosaves, atomic writes and full career history.
  • Portable careers. A single export file carries the save and any imported cover art, and Continue opens the career you played last.
  • Guided playtest harness. A bot plays whole careers across seeds and presets to catch dead ends and runaway economies.
  • Local problem reports. One click packages logs, the save and a private session timeline, including any unexpected errors, for a tester to send; nothing uploads by itself.
